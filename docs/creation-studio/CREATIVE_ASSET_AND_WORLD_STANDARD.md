# Creative Asset & World Design Standard

The child draws a rock once. They can name it “My Moon Potato”, move it across a scene, and choose it again for a game. Their marks should still be recognizable beside polished Jems scenery. A later drawing session or a different game role must leave their earlier adventure intact.

## Permanent art and 2D design decisions

Keep exact original bytes, including a camera image at its original dimensions, under the existing authorized owner. For authored drawings retain the recoverable editable source where available. Preserve hashes, parent revisions and the authored representation through backup and recovery. Any preparation produces a separate derivative. Permitted operations are crop, rotate, scale, flip, transparency, background removal and bounded compression. Optional edge smoothing requires a conservative deterministic algorithm, explicit selection and reversible edits. Preserve the drawn edges by default.

Jems never sends private artwork to a generative image model, redraws it, changes its artistic style, substitutes professional art, or labels generated or supplied content as a child's original work. This applies to previews, animations, games, exports, and every future model or adapter. Product illustrators may create Jems-owned worlds using approved product references.

New games use 2D sprites, layered scenes, and explicit movement and physics. Lighting, painterly perspective, shadows and optional restrained parallax belong to presentation. Existing retained games keep their compatible readers and runtimes. Scene coordinates, input, hit testing, collision and scoring must survive a change of scenery.

## Identity, version and use

| Question | Source of truth |
| --- | --- |
| Who owns it? | Active authorized profile and family context in the existing private drawing or scene authority |
| Which creation is this? | Stable original record, with validated parent links from its revisions |
| What does the child call it? | Editable child-facing name and optional semantic label such as “rock” or “moon potato” |
| Where could I find it? | Optional character, thing, vehicle or world category. Unclassified and imaginary creations remain usable |
| Which picture does this adventure show? | Exact selected image ID and content hash, or immutable rig ID and assembly hash binding the source |
| How does it move? | Child-authored rig parts, joints or movement instructions supported by the chosen activity |
| What is its job here? | Contextual scene or game use. A decoration, obstacle or collectible requires explicitly authored rules for any physical or scoring effect |
| Can I change it later? | Keep creates a revision. A later explicit selection can update a particular use. Previously kept projects retain their version history |

Bind ownership when opening a picker, validate again after asynchronous work, and abort on profile or scene change. Keep stock illustrations and earned Pal entitlements in their catalogues. A child may compose with both product art and private pictures while their provenance stays visible in stored references. Preserve original aspect, transparency and authored colors.

## Persistence and portability

My Art owns images, derived pictures, movement designs, working drafts and their usage records. Creative Studio owns actors, programs, layers, selected world, scene history and explicit Keep. The native game workspace owns game-specific bindings. Resolve references through these authorities and their existing transactional and recovery paths. Archive hides an item from new choices while retaining access for past adventures. Restoration validates complete content and links before writing. Additive import remaps related IDs together. Check the authoritative history before deleting. Older saved records retain their exact identifiers and compatibility behavior.

Working paper in a learning activity is transient and follows the activity's lifetime. Shared pointer, keyboard and undo patterns may be reused. Moving temporary work into the art collection requires a distinct explicit save, with the normal ownership and consent checks.

## Worlds and rendering

Each released world revision needs a stable catalogue ID, provenance, approval status, dimensions, digest, alpha policy, intended scale, layer and allowed use. A change to published artwork receives a new revision. Keep legacy backgrounds resolvable. Arrange scenery around the sides and horizon to give a moving pencil drawing or Pal a clear play area. Inspect the actual editor, playback, fullscreen, shelf, portable scene and supported recording path. Compare the stage at phone, tablet and desktop sizes with Gem Factory, Mind Your Jems!, My Art and the approved Pal/maker references.

The four initial illustrations depict Enchanted Gem Forest, Inventor's Workshop, Cloud Kingdom and Moon Adventure. Render their matching transparent props at child-selected positions. Any decoration rendered in front of an actor should occupy limited space. Visual depth has no authority over mechanics. Review the final composed stage before approving artwork.

## Game delivery, access and acceptance

The trusted host resolves private references. Generated game code receives no arbitrary owner identifier, private database access, source image bytes, credentials or unrestricted URL. Consent, trusted-device admission and review apply independently to family storage, private generation and paid dispatch. A future delivery mechanism needs scoped assets, independent security review, negative isolation tests and release qualification before production. A synthetic offline demonstration confers no production capability.

First steps keeps the code actions within reach. Follow the shared age-specific interface contract for touch targets, readable text, explicit narration and keyboard operation. Use visible errors and save recovery when a load, owner check or quota fails. Art choices do not earn learning scores or alter assistance, reward or Pal growth records.

Verify exact bytes and revision hashes through drawing, editing, use, export, restore and archive. Exercise new and old saves, owner changes, failed reads, missing art, explicit updates and offline recovery. Test visual composition separately from physics and deterministic challenge outcomes. Capture device dimensions and exact source with synthetic art. Track source implementation, offline evidence, hosted qualification, production deployment and external activation independently.
