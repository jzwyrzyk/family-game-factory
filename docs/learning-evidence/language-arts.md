# English / Language Arts — grade 1–4 benchmark and instrument specification

**Construct:** independent English reading, decoding/recognition, vocabulary and meaning, language conventions, comprehension and authentic writing. **Do not collapse reading, listening, and composing into one unsupported 'English score'.** For younger readers distinguish accuracy of decoding from ability to understand spoken directions. Tests should sample *new* connected texts, not game narration copied into quizzes.

## Evidence hierarchy
1. CCSS strand structure: `RL` literature, `RI` informational texts, `RF` foundational skills, `W` writing, `L` language, `SL` speaking/listening.
2. 2026 NAEP grade-4 reading framework: grade-4 comprehension design reference, not individual norm. Michigan K–2 benchmark / grades 3–4 M-STEP: authorized external outcomes.
3. IES K–3 foundational reading (decoding and connected text); IES K–3 comprehension; IES elementary writing process; grades 4–9 decoding/fluency/comprehension guide for grade-4 overlaps.
4. This design is **not** a DIBELS, IXL, MAP, Star, NAEP, or M-STEP replacement.

## Form A/B independent benchmark (proposed)
Each grade has two different original literary/informational passages; no repeated questions from games or previous forms. Use several short modules rather than one exhausting sitting, especially grades 1–2.

| Grade | Decoding/recognition 1pt | Grammar/spelling 1pt | Vocabulary 1pt | Comprehension 1pt | Original written response (4 traits x 0–2) | Max total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 8 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 8 | 29 |
| 2 | 6 | 5 | 4 | 7 | 8 | 30 |
| 3 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 9 | 8 | 31 |
| 4 | 3 | 5 | 5 | 10 | 8 | 31 |

Two passages per form (one narrative/literary, one informational); comprehension slots include **literal locate**, **integrate/infer**, and **evidence-based justification** at a grade-appropriate level. Text lengths, vocabulary load, read-aloud policy and writing expectations must be established with grade-level educator review; raw readability formula alone is not text-complexity validation.

### Separate outcomes, never one unvalidated composite
- **Foundational reading:** decoding and recognition first-pass accuracy; optionally **oral reading fluency (ORF)** in correctly read words per minute on *equated passages*, administered/proctored and scored under a licensed/validated protocol. Do **not** convert voice transcription speed/errors to ORF.
- **Reading comprehension:** score new passage questions with a fixed answer key/rubric; split literature and informational where enough items exist.
- **Language:** vocabulary-in-context and use of grammar/conventions.
- **Writing:** human-scored original composition; record prompt, modality, response and assistance; compare genres and prompt difficulty carefully.
- **Speaking/listening:** potential future strand requiring separate original oral comprehension/presentation protocol; speech input by itself does not measure oral proficiency.

### Writing rubric — 0/1/2 per dimension (total 0–8)
| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content/idea | No relevant coherent idea | Relevant idea, limited clarity | Clear idea or claim appropriate to prompt |
| Organization | No meaningful order | Partial organization | Grade-appropriate sequence/paragraph structure |
| Relevant development | No supporting details | One partially relevant detail | Appropriate relevant evidence/examples/details |
| Language clarity/conventions | Meaning largely inaccessible | Meaning understandable despite substantial errors | Understandable and mostly grade-appropriate sentence/convention use |

**Grade-specific anchors** must define what “appropriate” means. Grade 1 may earn full credit with brief complete idea and one detail; grade 4 needs connected elaboration and organizing structure. A grade-1 6/8 is not a grade-4 6/8. To claim improvement, writing prompts and scoring anchors must be matched and double-coded blind to timepoint. Do not use an LLM as the sole evaluator of official results. Preserve blinded adjudication for disagreements; evaluate inter-rater agreement.

### Example response stimuli (original; demonstration only)
- Grade 1: “Write two sentences telling a friend what animal you would like to care for and why.” Optional drawings supported but record whether visual details influenced the writing score.
- Grade 2: “Describe how to plant a seed. Explain at least two steps in order.”
- Grade 3: “Explain which of two ways to reduce litter in a park would help more, with two reasons.”
- Grade 4: “A school is considering a quiet reading room. Write an opinion that gives a clear position and reasons, then check whether your paragraphs are organized.”

### Passage construction protocol
- Prepare a controlled matrix of literary and nonfiction topics by grade; avoid knowledge and cultural experience that inadvertently dominate the score.
- Author **original** texts and verify non-infringement. Maintain `passage_id, version, grade, word_count, syntax_features, vocabulary profile, reviewer` and item-to-passage associations.
- All independent comprehension passages withheld from coaching, generative prompts, analytics examples and public frontend bundles.
- For reading-comprehension assessment **do not read the passage aloud** unless measuring listening comprehension and reporting it separately. An adult may neutrally read the directions. Maintain standardized accessible accommodations and separate labels.
- Grade 1 assessment may be divided into shorter modules with breaks. Don't require typing speed to demonstrate comprehension; use selection/pointing for non-writing domains.
- Use balanced topics, accessible type and screen-reader support; if interaction itself impedes the child, flag measurement invalid rather than mark incorrect.

## Quality / school comparability gates
- **Content validity:** literacy specialist independently reviews every item and passage; grade benchmarks and recommended text complexity checked.
- **Parallel-form validity:** two alternate passages and prompts with matched constructs; eventually calibrate item difficulty/discrimination and examine subgroup fairness. Simple raw % from form A vs B is not yet normed growth.
- **Convergent validity:** with permission, correlate with existing K–2 school benchmarks or grades 3–4 M-STEP/qualified outside reading measure; evaluate reading vs writing separately.
- **Avoid construct contamination:** reading tasks measure reading, not listening; writing tasks measure child writing, not LLM editing; sentence explanations assisted by a parent invalidate independent evidence.
- **User experience:** child-facing no “below grade level” labels, optional pause/resume consistent with administration, secure test not available to child coaching.
- **Existing catalog:** `src/language/curriculum.ts` emphasizes phonics/spelling/vocabulary and shorter comprehension exercises; extended authentic reading/writing and speaking/listening require new assessments. Do not infer full grade competency from the current practice bank.
