# Mathematics — grade 1–4 benchmark and instrument specification

**Status:** design blueprint; no Jems score is currently equivalent to Common Core proficiency, Michigan benchmark scale, NAEP, M-STEP, IXL or MAP Growth. This is a grade-level construct blueprint, **not a clause-by-clause certification of every CCSS standard**. Full standard-ID crosswalk and educator sign-off are required before such a claim.

## Construct
Assess independently demonstrated (A) concepts and representations, (B) procedural accuracy/fluency, (C) applying math to unfamiliar situations, (D) explanation/strategic reasoning. The existing 73-unit Jems math catalog supplies *practice objectives*, not test items or normed skill estimates. Mathematical Practices (MP.1–MP.8) are integrated via reasoning rubrics, not automatically credited because a child completed a game.

### Fidelity to school expectations
- Build a **machine-readable crosswalk** of *all* official standard IDs by grade with explicit statuses: `covered-in-practice`, `sampled-in-test`, `untested`, `out-of-scope`, `requires-educator-review`. Never treat the Jems supplemental curriculum as a full school curriculum.
- **No composite across grades** until equating and validated scale linking exist. A grade-3 70% and grade-4 70% do not imply equal underlying ability.
- Corroboration: authorized Michigan grades 1–2 benchmark and grade 3–4 M-STEP data; grade 4 NAEP framework for design reference (not personalized NAEP scores).
- The eight CCSS mathematical practices should appear through unfamiliar, constructed-response transfer items: making sense of problems, reasoning quantities, arguments, modeling, tools, precision, structure, repeated reasoning.

## Form blueprints — two alternate forms (A/B), same slots and rubric

**Construction requirements:** roughly 1/3 conceptual/representational, 1/3 procedural, 1/3 novel application across form; balance topics and reading load; avoid accidental science/reading knowledge dependencies; build distinct but matched A/B items and an additional withheld C for delayed transfer; no production generative item or answer-key retrieval. Test forms must be locked, versioned, manually reviewed and eventually calibrated.

### Response scoring
- Auto-score objective items using exact numerical/equivalent-answer oracle; accept equivalent fractions/units and unambiguous alternate formatting where allowed. Do not mark dysgraphia/typing error as mathematical misconception without a supported accommodations pathway.
- Constructed story/justification: **0** incorrect/no valid relationship; **1** correct mathematical relationship or strategy with partial result/explanation; **2** accurate result *and* mathematically valid strategy/representation. Each item has an *item-specific* scoring guide and sample anchors; trained, blinded human raters score the research forms.
- Log the first committed answer separately from hints, second tries or worked examples. **Content, strategy or answer help disqualifies the affected response from an independent post-test score**, though it can count as coached formative practice. Record permitted neutral instruction reading and other access support separately, keep it consistent with the construct and administration protocol, and do not equate an absent app hint with verified independence.
- Report strand % with (n) and raw marks; **do not call 80% “on grade level.”** That cut score would need defensible standard setting and empirical validation. For sparse strand samples (`n<5`) do not display a mastery badge.
- Optional fluency: separate, brief, grade-valid measure with response accuracy and rate; do not penalize un-timed conceptual questions by speed. Document timed-task accommodations.

### Rubric anchored math examples (original; public examples only)
- G1: “There are 9 shells on a table. Four are moved into a box. How many remain?” Answer 5; ask for a drawing or expression for the 2-point reasoning slot.
- G2: “A jar holds 128 beads. Add 50, then remove 13. How many?” Answer 165. Correct operation sequence matters.
- G3: “A rectangle has 4 rows of 7 square tiles. What is its area?” Answer 28 square units; accept array evidence.
- G4: “A field is 6 m long and 4 m wide. A fence goes all around it. How long is the fence?” Answer 20 m; perimeter not area.

## Grade boundary and accommodations
- Parent-selected grade is an *instruction ceiling*, not a test-based disability/ability label. Assess actual instructed objectives and optionally adjacent prerequisites; do not present a grade-wide academic conclusion from a narrow skill check.
- G1 must allow a trained adult neutral instruction reading; any spoken item reading must not reveal solution logic. Record accommodation type and apply consistently at all time points.
- If test is incomplete, mark *incomplete* and keep denominator fixed; never silently score abandoned items as wrong or remove incorrect responses.
- For non-English-dominant students, distinguish numerical reasoning from English reading load; do not translate a standardized form without recalibrating it.
- Math device/drag manipulations must be accessible via keyboard/touch and should not determine correctness when motor skills are incidental.
