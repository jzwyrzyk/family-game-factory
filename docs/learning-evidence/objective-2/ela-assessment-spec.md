# English / Language Arts independent instrument — grade 1–4

**Status: provisional authored assessment blueprint, NOT a validated reading or writing diagnostic.** Source: Michigan's 2022 K–2 ELA blueprint, Michigan M-STEP Math/ELA blueprint March 2021, Common Core grade strands and IES foundational reading/writing guidance.

### Critical validity principle
Language Arts is **multidimensional**. A correct vocabulary-choice answer does *not* prove a child can read unfamiliar connected text. Listening to a read-aloud passage does *not* prove independent reading. Dictating to an LLM does *not* prove independent writing. A polished AI-produced game narrative is not evidence of the child's writing.

## B. Reading tasks — exact construct separation
Each form has at least one original literary and informational text, with new characters/subject across A/B/C; ideally two texts of each genre for varied sampling once feasibility tested. Assess:
1. **Literal retrieval:** identify accurately a fact actually stated.
2. **Integration/inference:** use more than one clue rather than an unrelated outside assumption.
3. **Vocabulary in context:** use context, word-part knowledge and multiple meanings, distinct from short spelling quizzes.
4. **Structure/evidence:** explain sequence, cause/effect, compare sources, identify a detail that supports a claim.
5. **Upper grade:** compare perspectives, summarize main idea/theme, judge evidence's appropriateness.

**Passage design:** grade-specific vocabulary, syntax, length, cohesion and background-knowledge load; culturally diverse but fair scenarios. A readability formula does not establish grade suitability. Original fiction and nonfiction only; no copied protected prose. Log text complexity features, word count, reviewer, original content author, version and source. Multiple questions on one passage are **locally dependent** and not 15 psychometrically independent observations; calibration should account for passage/testlet effects.

**Administration:** If measuring independent reading, **do not read the stimulus aloud**, even if neutral directions are read. An accessible read-aloud accommodation alters the construct and is reported distinctly (listening comprehension), with separate comparable norms if later available. Children with reading disabilities need individualized accessible alternatives; report exactly what was assessed, never force an unsupported reading label.

## C. Foundational reading / spelling (G1–G2 and separately older grades)
- Grade 1: letter-sound associations, phoneme segmentation and blending, CVC/CVCC decoding, common digraphs, irregular words, sentence recognition.
- Grade 2: two-syllable words, vowel teams and morphemes, inflectional endings, frequent irregular spelling and early connected-text fluency.
- Grade 3–4 optional: multisyllabic decoding, affixes and appropriately proctored word recognition/ORF.
- **Explicit score limitations:** written multiple-choice recognition items are not an independent oral decoding measure; phonemic awareness requires auditory tasks; novel pseudo-words/regular word decoding need expert-designed item sampling. Voice transcription is an interaction layer, not a valid oral-reading score.

## D. Original prose examples (PUBLIC demonstrations — not secure scored items)

### Grade 1 literary example, ~70 words (original)
> Jalen made a kite from yellow paper. He tied on a short string and ran across the grass. The kite rose for a moment, then fell beside a tree. Jalen noticed that the string had come loose. He asked his grandmother for help tying a stronger knot. When he tried again, the kite stayed high. Jalen smiled and watched it turn above the field.

Candidate questions: What did Jalen make? Why did it fall? What did he change? What detail shows the second try worked? Only questions with an unambiguous, text-supported answer become objective keyed items.

### Grade 2 informational example, ~100 words (original)
> A community garden can help people grow vegetables in a small area. Some gardeners plant beans beside a fence because the stems can climb. Others put lettuce in a spot with morning sun and afternoon shade. Gardeners check the soil before watering: wet soil may not need more water that day. In summer, volunteers record how much rain has fallen, so they can compare dry and wet weeks. A small sign reminds visitors to leave paths clear for wheelbarrows. Everyone has a job, from pulling weeds to collecting the harvest. The garden works best when volunteers plan and share the work.

Sample comprehension: What helps beans climb? Why does the group record rainfall? Which action is an example of planning? Which statement is supported by the passage, not merely plausible?

### Grade 3 literary example, ~125 words (original)
> Noor wanted the classroom's new board game to be fair for everyone. At first, her classmates suggested that the oldest child should always go first. Noor thought this would leave some people waiting too often. She drew six numbered squares on scraps of paper and placed them in a cup. Every player could pick a square, and the smallest number would go first. The class tested the idea during three rounds. People got different turns each time, but one child noticed that the cup had two copies of the number four. Noor replaced the extra four with a missing number and tested the game again. Her classmates agreed that checking the pieces before playing was part of making a fair game.

Questions should distinguish *explicit detail* (a duplicate four), inference (why people found the change fairer), and textual evidence (the reason the pieces were checked). This is a reading test, **not** an APS score.

### Grade 4 informational example, ~150 words (original)
> Two neighborhoods wanted to reduce heat around their playgrounds. The first planted young shade trees. The second installed a temporary fabric canopy above a seating area. On a sunny afternoon, both groups measured the temperature of benches in shaded and unshaded spots. They recorded the same type of bench, the time of day, and how long each seat had been in the sun. The canopy offered shade immediately, but the fabric needed periodic repair. The trees were less effective during the first year, yet they were expected to cover a larger area as they grew. Both groups wanted to compare measurements again in the following summer, since one afternoon would not capture all weather conditions. They also asked families where they usually sat, because a shaded bench would not help much if nobody used it. Their final report weighed short-term cooling, maintenance, and how people used the playground.

Questions: identify the claim, compare the two strategies, explain why one observation is insufficient and select evidence supporting a tradeoff.

**Do not reuse these public passages for pre/post or claim they meet a particular grade text-complexity band without expert verification.** Secure A/B/C passages must be original, materially different and withheld from Jems coaching and game generation.

## E. Independent writing rubrics (0–8 per task)
All writing tasks have clear purpose, audience and genre, with a matched but novel prompt at pre/post. Four dimensions scored 0–2, grade-relative, by **two blinded independent raters**:
| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Idea/claim | No coherent relevant idea | Relevant idea, unclear/incomplete | Clearly communicates grade-appropriate purpose |
| Organization | Disordered/unstructured | Some sequence/paragraph organization | Coherent beginning/development/ending, grade-appropriate |
| Development/evidence | No relevant specifics | Limited support/examples | Sufficient relevant details and explanations |
| Language/conventions | Meaning generally not intelligible | Meaning understandable with frequent errors | Grade-appropriate clarity and conventions |

**G1 full-credit example:** one or two coherent sentences giving an opinion with a reason, not a grade 4 multi-paragraph essay. **G4 full-credit example:** organized opinion/explanation with multiple relevant pieces of evidence, coherence and appropriate transitions. **Neither grade's raw 8-point total means the same academic level.**

Constructed task ideas: G1 "Tell a friend about a kind action and why it mattered"; G2 "Explain how to care for a class plant in order"; G3 "Recommend a school recess activity and support it with reasons"; G4 "Compare two choices for a community project with relevant evidence and explain the tradeoff." Those are **public demonstrators only**. Assessor must use prompt-specific anchor exemplars. Draft → revision assessments should distinguish original child writing from parent/AI supplied text.

## F. Research and source integration (grades 3–4)
The goal is **evidence handling from provided texts or charts**, not internet browsing. Provide two original, short, distinct sources with identifiable origin/context, then ask which source answers a question, how the claims compare, which evidence is stronger and why. One constructed response has a four-part 0–8 rubric:
- `question_source`: identifies the inquiry and appropriate source(s).
- `evidence_selection`: chooses relevant accurate evidence, not unrelated facts.
- `integration`: connects/composes information from two sources without contradiction.
- `communication`: gives a clear, traceable explanation with enough context.

Each is scored 0 (absent/wrong), 1 (partly demonstrated), 2 (clearly independently demonstrated). Include actual source text and question-level anchor answers in the **protected bank**, never publicly.

## G. Audio listening items (grades 3–4)
At least one original narrated story or informational audio stimulus per form. Eight audio-only response items are aligned to `SL.3.* / SL.4.*`. The test question and choices can be shown visually as authorized, but the actual listening stimulus is never displayed as text in the test. Locked audio version, playback/rewind policy, controlled volume, headphone/accommodation rules and device check must be consistent. An audio clip absent/failed means **technical invalid**, not a wrong answer. Browser speech recognition / microphone permission does not administer listening comprehension.

## H. Known measurement limits and minimum quality work
- ELA short-text exact matching needs author-defined accepted alternatives; punctuation/capitalization differences may be acceptable depending on target. No LLM invented synonym scoring. If the construct is composition, use human rubric, not exact-match.
- Writing and research have rater disagreement and anchored adjudication; no unreviewed AI scoring.
- Passage comprehension, oral word reading, writing, listening and research **must remain separate reported domains** until a supported composite model exists.
- Grade-valid variation in text length/difficulty, socioeconomic/linguistic bias, and alternate-form comparability require observed student data; not inferred from designer intuition.
- An oral/language accommodation may change which skill was measured; record it and prevent silent cross-mode growth comparison.
- Separate original audio and source licensing; children's written responses stored only with future approved guardian consent/retention.
- No claims about IXL/M-STEP/NAEP equivalence, grade level or causation.
