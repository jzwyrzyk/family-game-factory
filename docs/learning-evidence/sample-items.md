# Original *public demonstration* mini-forms and scoring anchors

**DANGER — THESE EXAMPLES ARE PUBLICLY EXPOSED, NOT VALIDATED, NOT SECRET AND NOT SUITABLE FOR PRE/POST IMPACT MEASUREMENT.** These are original Jems-authored *prototypes* to make the specifications reviewable. They **do not constitute complete alternate Forms A/B**, nor a standardized grade-placement test. Required secure A/B/C item banks must be written, reviewed and calibrated independently in protected storage. All item texts are original; no copying of third-party proprietary test questions.

## Administration
Neutral adult directions only; do not tell the child correct responses or supply strategies. Use age-appropriate independent response and note accommodations. These are paper review examples, not an online assessment. Domain scoring cannot be merged into an overall "learning IQ."

**Missing is not zero.** Every zero anchor below describes a valid, observed response that does not meet the criterion. An unanswered/skipped step, no opportunity, interruption or technical failure is missing/invalid (`null`), with a reason. Incomplete scenarios or forms have no overall total. Record support at each decision; permitted neutral access support is distinct from content/strategy/answer help, and an absent app hint is not verified independence. These demonstrations use the research-only human scoring and administration rules in [assessment-scoring.md](assessment-scoring.md).

## 2. ELA original passage and sample questions per grade
These passages are authored only as **short demos**; educator review of decoding difficulty, passage length, syntax and cultural relevance is outstanding. **Do not use** them for a national literacy score or pre/post evidence. Questions 1–3 one point each; writing prompt 0–8 using the four-criterion rubric in [language-arts.md](language-arts.md). These demos omit full decoding/conventions/vocabulary coverage required in the real blueprint.

### Grade 1 — *The Red Scarf*
> Mina found a red scarf beside the gate. She asked two neighbors whose scarf it was. Mr. Lee said it belonged to his dog, Pip. Mina returned the scarf. Pip wagged his tail.

1. Where did Mina find the scarf? **Key:** beside the gate.
2. Who owned the scarf? **Key:** Pip / Mr. Lee's dog.
3. What shows that Pip was happy? **Key:** he wagged his tail.
4. Writing (0–8): Write two sentences about how you could help a neighbor.

### Grade 2 — *The Class Garden*
> Every morning, Taro checked the class garden. One Monday, the leaves looked droopy. He touched the soil and found it dry. Taro asked his teacher if the class could water the plants. After two days, the leaves looked upright again. The class put a reminder beside the window so they would check the soil even on busy mornings.

1. What problem did Taro notice? **Key:** droopy leaves/dry soil.
2. Why did the plants likely improve? **Key:** watering/soil no longer dry.
3. What is the main purpose of the reminder? **Key:** help the class remember to check soil/water.
4. Writing (0–8): Explain two steps for taking care of a plant, in order.

### Grade 3 — *The Shady Corner*
> A class noticed that puddles lasted longer in one corner of the playground. After the next rain, students measured the puddle in the sunny area and the shaded area each morning. The sunny puddle became much smaller by the next day. The shaded puddle changed only a little. The students wanted to know whether shade was the only reason, so they planned to check whether both areas had the same type of ground.

1. What question were the students investigating? **Key:** why puddles last longer in one location / role of shade.
2. Which evidence shows a difference between locations? **Key:** sunny puddle shrank more than shaded.
3. Why should the students examine the ground? **Key:** different ground may also affect puddle drying; test other explanation.
4. Writing (0–8): Explain what the students should do next and why, using evidence from the passage. **Avoid double-crediting APS; this is an ELA written explanation on text evidence.**

### Grade 4 — *A Place to Read*
> The school council wanted to create a new place where children could read during recess. Some students suggested a bench under the largest tree, because it would be shaded and quiet. Others proposed a room near the gym, which would remain usable in rainy weather. The council collected survey responses and counted how often the proposed outdoor spot was wet after storms. The bench was less expensive, but the indoor room would cost more to prepare. The group decided to write a recommendation after comparing how many children would use each option, how much each would cost, and when it could be used.

1. Name **two** criteria the council considered. **Key:** any two of cost, student use, weather availability, shade, quiet; one point requires two.
2. Which plan could still be used during rain? **Key:** room near gym.
3. Why did the council not choose a plan immediately? **Key:** they wanted evidence about costs, usage and weather before deciding.
4. Writing (0–8): Recommend one option, cite two relevant passage details, and explain a tradeoff.

**Human writing scoring:** 0–2 each for idea, organization, relevant detail, clarity/conventions; use grade-specific anchors and blind duplicate ratings before field use.

## 3. Computational Thinking sample four-item mini-forms per grade
All logic/puzzle prompts are original and **unplugged**, with one point per item. These are text stand-ins for age-appropriate diagrams; the final forms require independently checked diagrams, distractors and accessible versions. Answer positions are not used as a skill.

### Grade 1
1. **Sequence:** Arrange the cards “put on socks”, “put on shoes” to get dressed. **Key:** socks then shoes.
2. **Pattern:** Circle, square, circle, square, __. **Key:** circle.
3. **Decompose:** To make a paper airplane, which two things must you do? **Key:** get paper and fold it; distractors must be independently designed.
4. **Debug:** A toy is meant to move forward twice. Its card row says forward, back. Which card should change? **Key:** back -> forward.

### Grade 2
1. **Sequence:** From (0,0) on an empty grid, move right then up then right. Where are you? **Key:** (2,1).
2. **Pattern:** Repeating moves: up, right, up, right... What is move 7? **Key:** up.
3. **Decompose:** To deliver a letter, name two subgoals. **Key:** get/address letter, take it to correct destination; item must allow defensible alternatives.
4. **Debug:** A robot must move right three times; cards are R, R, L. Which card is wrong? **Key:** third, should be R.

### Grade 3
1. **Sequence:** Follow east, east, north from (0,0). Final coordinate? **Key:** (2,1).
2. **Condition:** Rule: red = stop, other colors = move 1. Red, green, green. Moves? **Key:** 2.
3. **Decompose:** Build a simple maze game: which two independent systems can you design first? **Key:** player movement and goal detection; accept equivalent decomposition.
4. **Debug:** A robot must reach (2,1) from (0,0), but cards R, U, L lead to (0,1). Which one card can change? **Key:** third L -> R.

### Grade 4
1. **Algorithm:** Repeat “right, up” three times from (0,0). Final coordinate? **Key:** (3,3).
2. **Condition:** If a square is blocked, turn right; otherwise go forward. On inputs blocked, clear, blocked, how many forward steps? **Key:** 1 (assume each input produces only its named operation).
3. **Decompose:** A maze game requires movement, obstacles and scoring. Name a useful two-stage plan and why. **Key:** any valid independent grouping such as movement/obstacles first, scoring next; human-scored 0/1 until deterministic rubric designed.
4. **Debug:** From (0,0) target is (2,1). Planned R,U,R works. Cards actually say R,U,D. Which card must change? **Key:** final D -> R.

**Limitations:** several decomposition prototypes are open response; independent evaluation requires human scoring or conversion to unambiguous selection items. Coordinate puzzles require pilot review for age suitability and visual layout. **Do not use these mixed-format samples as a 4-point calibrated scale.**

## 4. Active Problem-Solving grade-specific scenario mini-probes
One original scenario per grade; score six 0–3 dimensions according to [active-problem-solving.md](active-problem-solving.md); **0–18 max for a complete scenario only**, not a national scaled score. Missing relevant opportunities or invalid responses withhold the total. The full independent instrument requires **two diverse scenarios per form** and new held-out tasks.

| Grade | Scenario | Child actions required and observer evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Paper bridge**: two bridge shapes carry same toy; one bends | say goal; choose stronger bridge; explain evidence; try a new change and say what would happen with heavier toy |
| 2 | **Leaking watering can**: two lids are tested; one drips 5 times and one only once under matched trials | define goal, propose alternative, pick what to count, interpret results, improve, transfer to another container |
| 3 | **Two ramps**: balls and ramp heights both change between tests | explain why cause cannot be isolated, plan one-variable comparison, interpret given trials, redesign |
| 4 | **Outdoor reading area**: Option A shaded/cheap but unusable in rain; Option B weatherproof/costly | define at least two success criteria and budget constraint; compare options, plan evidence collection, interpret usage/cost figures, suggest revision, apply principle to another school choice |

### Rubric for each scenario (all grades, with separate grade-specific anchors)
| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Define goal | Stated goal misses the task | vague | concrete success | concrete plus relevant constraint/grade-valid detail |
| Options | Chosen option does not address the goal | one weak | viable alternative(s) | reasoned comparison against goal |
| Test plan | Proposed test cannot inform the question | arbitrary | relevant observation/prediction | fair, justified comparison (older grades) |
| Evidence | Observed explanation contradicts or does not use results | repeats superficial result | relates result to goal | compares and recognizes limitation |
| Revision | irrelevant | untargeted | fixes a cause supported by result | improves and explains next test |
| Transfer | Proposed application does not fit the new situation | same words | applies method to new situation | explains how it might need adapting |

**Grade-1 full-credit must be defined relative to age:** a child can earn a 3 through a clear choice, simple relevant explanation and valid comparison; do not demand grade-4 engineering vocabulary. Not suitable for independent scoring without detailed annotated child responses.
