# Gem Pal design doctrine

**Scope:** durable creative requirements for Jems companion design. Read [Studio](STUDIO.md) for source selection and [Growth](GROWTH_GUIDE.md) for the second form. This guide does not claim production integration or a measured improvement in image generation.

## 1. Write one connected brief

Connect the decisions in this order:

**Borrowed principle → body structure → gem mechanism → useful action → personality → growth.**

Use a compact sentence: “This creature has [body structure] because it [action]; its [gem system] makes that possible; it is [temperament], except [funny contradiction]. In its grown form, [structural development] lets it [new capability].”

Treat this as a design test, not story copy to print on the image. If the causal links are weak, revise before rendering. A charming paragraph cannot repair a body that fails to communicate its premise.

Use selected Jems references to establish material, lighting, face language, finish, and appeal. Do not substitute a generic imitation of a named animation studio or collectible-monster franchise. Share a visual family without forcing one body, facial proportion, or temperament on every species.

## 2. Borrow a principle and create a creature

Explore unrelated domains: tools, household mechanisms, instruments, paper structures, packaging, woven materials, seeds, water behavior, or scientific devices. Use a primary principle and add a second only when it earns its place. Research unfamiliar physical mechanisms with appropriate primary sources when accuracy matters; distinguish real observations from fictional abilities.

Record each source:

| Field | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Stable source ID, object/domain, source URL or original reference, date |
| Observation | The object's actual structure and action |
| Borrowed properties | What the design takes, without copying the whole object |
| Creature interpretation | Body rationale, gem job, movement, useful action |
| Character contrast | Funny limitation or mismatch in temperament |
| Roster overlap | Nearest species and meaningful differences |

These S–X design examples illustrate the method. They describe the premise, not current asset approval or runtime support; resolve selections through the reference route in [Studio](STUDIO.md).

| Species | Inspiration | Body and gem relationship | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Poff | Bellows | Low pleated torso; paired violet valve crystals | Compresses and directs a gentle puff |
| Chimi | Kalimba | Lilac crescent; separate graduated gem tines | Produces notes and an occasional comic wrong note |
| Pleeko | Pop-up engineering | Peach folded structure; emerald hinges | Unfolds to provide reach or steps |
| Talli | Retractable tape | Blue ribbon structure; coral endpoint gem | Extends, compares, and recoils |
| Zippa | Zipper | Paired magenta mantle edges; discrete gold gem rows | Joins pieces through an interlocking path |
| Whorl | Gyroscope | Open aqua body; supported sapphire rotor | Balances through rotation |

Do not treat this table as sufficient visual reference for reproduction, or infer that the actions are implemented in the product.

Compare each candidate with its three closest roster neighbors across silhouette, locomotion, gem topology, gem mechanism, and social role. As an editorial default, seek differences in at least three dimensions, including silhouette and either mechanism or movement. This is a design aid, not an objective originality score. Reject a familiar-looking concept even if it technically checks three boxes.

Reusing inspiration is allowed when its interpretation changes meaningfully. Changing palette, animal type, or gem color alone does not establish a new Pal. Record discarded concepts so later batches do not repeat weak combinations.

## 3. Design gem anatomy deliberately

Specify count, relative size, shape family, placement, material, attachment, motion, and role. Lock important geometry for a specific approved form; do not impose one catalog-wide recipe.

| Arrangement | Opportunity | Review risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One dominant gem | Focal point, rotor, anchor, lens, floating focus | Generic jewelry; unsupported mass; obscured face |
| Matched pair | Valves, balanced supports, paired functions | Unintended asymmetry or missing counterpart |
| Graduated comb/array | Resonance, ordered steps, sequence | Gems merge into ears, horns, or skin lobes |
| Many tiny gems | Joining, weaving, distributed action | Dots, teeth, bumps, confetti, or illegibility |
| Structural gem segments | Hinges, braces, rails, integrated architecture | Decorative armor obscures the mechanism |
| Deliberately suspended gem | Magic, orbit, relationship, focused control | Accidental detachment or extra character |

One large gem is not inherently better than several small ones. Use relative scale to support function and silhouette. Inspect at intended display size; do not invent universal pixel or percentage ratios. Record measured ratios only for specific approved forms when useful.

Make material separation legible: firm facets and controlled crystal reflections; the selected softer or matte body treatment; clear sockets, roots, supports, or purposeful gaps. Integration still needs a visible boundary. Keep tiny crystals discrete rather than rendering glossy skin nodules.

Explain support logic. A terminal stone needs counterbalance; a rotor needs clearance; folds need connected joints; a lens must fit its facial construction. Fantasy may suspend a gem intentionally, as with Navi, without adding an axle that destroys the premise. Preserve that fantasy rule across poses.

Ask: **If the gem stopped working, what useful action would change?** A purely ornamental answer requires stronger design. This is a fictional causal test, not a demand for scientifically realistic creatures.

## 4. Make the Base form complete

Give Base a satisfying standalone silhouette, face, purposeful gem action, personality, and reason to be selected. Do not make it unfinished so all appeal is reserved for growth. Let future development be latent in its anatomy or behavior.

Record family anchors separately from Base anatomy. Family anchors may include facial construction, signature color relationships, gem/body contrast, a particular gem relationship, temperament, or movement principle. Base anatomy records actual proportions, limbs, joints, crystal topology, and face placement. Distinguish anchors from flexible design options.

Keep story short: a useful role plus a comic habit. A careful measurer believes it can reach a little farther; an earnest musician decides a funny wrong note belongs in the song. Humor should arise from this species' mechanism, rather than repeating one generic joke.

Plan a simple game use: an action, prop interaction, movement, or expression compatible with existing capabilities. Record advanced mechanics separately as proposals. Suggested abilities do not establish runtime support.
