# Gem Pal quality gates

Start with [STUDIO.md](STUDIO.md), apply the [design doctrine](DESIGN_DOCTRINE.md) and [growth guide](GROWTH_GUIDE.md), and record decisions through [CONTINUITY.md](CONTINUITY.md). These checks assess artwork; they do not establish runtime support or production readiness.

## Record an observable outcome

Inspect the actual candidate and selected reference. A prompt, filename, or description is not evidence that the image meets its brief. Give each applicable check a result and a short reason:

| Result | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Pass** | Inspected evidence meets the requirement. This is internal QA, not owner approval. |
| **Revise** | The identity and direction are usable, with a specific defect to repair. |
| **Reject** | The candidate uses the wrong identity or direction, or needs a different structural approach. |
| **Not verified** | A required reference, image inspection, or comparison is unavailable. State what is missing. |

Keep concept selection, exact-art approval, expressions, animation, integration, and in-game verification separate. Do not convert missing evidence into Pass or average a defining failure into a passing score.

## 1. Identity and selected reference

Resolve the character, form, variant, exact reference, and revision. Board letters and similar names cannot establish identity. An older explicitly selected image takes precedence over a newer unselected candidate. Preserve superseded alternatives in history without using them as masters.

For a local edit, inspect that form's reference. For growth, inspect Base and the selected Grown reference when one exists. If a reference is unavailable, record the selection as pending and continue independent work on resolved targets. Do not recreate a missing master from its description and present it as the original.

## 2. Base quality and construction

Confirm a distinctive silhouette, understandable personality, useful action, and purposeful gem arrangement. Inspect intended limb count, connected joints, facial construction, overlap, support, and movement clearance.

Crystals need readable facets and material separation from the body. Check sockets, roots, mounts, or deliberate gaps. Tiny gems must remain crystals rather than dots, teeth, or glossy skin bumps. One dominant gem, paired gems, combs, and small arrays are valid when they support the species' purpose. Do not impose a universal gem count or size ratio.

Preserve intentional fantasy. Navi's deliberate floating-gem relationship is not an attachment defect; accidental disconnected limbs or fragments are. Check the selected species' construction rather than forcing ordinary mechanics onto every design.

## 3. Meaningful growth

Review two total forms: Base and Grown, one transition. The artifact registry's keys remain `forms.base` and `forms.mature`; **Grown** is the display label for the mature form.

Compare forms at the same displayed height and similar framing. Require visible development in architecture, articulation, stance, proportions, negative space, or movement, with recognizable family anchors. Enlargement, recoloring, glow, costume, or an extra prop alone is insufficient.

Keep structural gems in the silhouette comparison. Mentally remove decorative effects, not the crystal geometry that defines the body. The new capability must be supported by what the image shows. A stronger story cannot compensate for unchanged anatomy.

## 4. Composition and practical readability

Use one character instance per individual asset. Exclude duplicate poses, baby insets, helper creatures, and accidental reflections. Keep defining features within the frame. A review layout may place separately prepared Base and Grown assets side by side.

Compare the candidate with its nearest roster neighbors for silhouette, movement, gem topology, mechanism, and role. Palette alone does not establish a distinct species. Inspect full size for defects and the intended interface size for readability. Unspecified thumbnail dimensions are review choices, not established product requirements.

## 5. Expressions and scoped corrections

Resolve each form's master before producing its expression set. Preserve face and lens construction, gem relationships, anatomy, proportions, and materials. Change emotion or pose deliberately; do not redesign the species with every expression.

Carry forward specific corrections at their proper scope. A corrected arm attachment applies to that character and form; a general attachment check applies across the roster. Preserve character-specific lenses, wings, and gem behavior from their references without turning them into universal anatomy rules. Recheck affected expressions and rigs after structural edits.

## Reactions when something goes wrong

Distinguish an artwork defect from an intentional reaction to an error. The following vocabulary is a **proposed working set**, not blanket approval for every Pal:

| Situation | Suggested direction |
| --- | --- |
| Incorrect answer | Friendly curiosity with a clear next step. |
| Retry or hint | Attentive, character-specific encouragement. |
| Harmless Pal mistake | Brief self-directed humor. |
| Technical failure | Calm waiting; interface text explains the issue. |
| Success after effort | Celebration connected to the Pal's signature action. |
| Thinking or listening | Readable attention without anatomy drift. |

Inspect each output individually. Record exact candidate, checks, unresolved defects, decision, and downstream impact. Fix clear generation defects before presenting artwork for creative review.
